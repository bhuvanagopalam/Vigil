What we built

How a phone measures it

Pulse. The flash lights the fingertip and the camera watches the glow. Each heartbeat pushes more blood into the finger, which absorbs more light, so the red channel dips once per beat.

20 seconds of that is a pulse waveform, the same signal as a hospital finger clip. Beat spacing gives heart rate, its variation gives HRV, the slow swell under the beats gives breathing.

Real trace from this phone: 6 seconds, 72 beats per minute.

Capillary refill. Press the finger hard on the lens for 4 seconds: blood is squeezed out and the red brightens. Relax without lifting: blood floods back and the red falls.

Time from release to 90% of the way back is the refill time. Slow refill means poor circulation, an early sign of shock. A nurse times it by eye in whole seconds. The camera times it in hundredths.

Real trace from this phone: release, then refill in 0.7 seconds.

Scan. 30 seconds. You're on the board.
